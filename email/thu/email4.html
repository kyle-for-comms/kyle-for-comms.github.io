Hi IOP,

Today's brick is accountability. The IOP does a lot of great work, but it rarely tells you how that work went: who came, what you thought, or what changed because of it.

And at last night's debate, I talked about writing the IOP an operating manual to make it easier for new and prospective members to navigate the large organization. Today it's live on my site. Here's what's in it.

THREE PAGES, ONE FROM EACH
01Didn't get in? Ask why.
Programs that pick members have to grade every application and interview on the same standard criteria. After decisions come out, any applicant can ask the chair to walk through how they did against those criteria.
02Bring your own idea.
Any Harvard undergrad can bring a proposal to SAC. Send it in writing to the IOP President at least one week before the next meeting. The President and Vice President will decide whether it goes to a vote.
03Off the record means off the record.
Small rooms work because guests can speak candidly. Unless you've been told otherwise, ask before you quote, record or post anything from a study group, a dinner or office hours.
